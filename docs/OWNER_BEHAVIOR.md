# Owner Behavior

## Model

An owner has hidden persistent traits and visible runtime states.

Persistent traits: patience, baseline anxiety, observation, medical literacy, trust, conflict tendency, financial flexibility, honesty, comprehension and adherence.

Runtime states: irritation, anxiety, trust, understanding, consent and willingness to follow the plan. Anxiety, irritation and distrust are distinct and require different responses.

Appearance, age, gender, clothing and skin color are generated independently from behavior.

## Humor

Humor targets human explanations, contradictions, clinic chaos and animal behavior, never the suffering animal. Three levels are used:

1. common everyday phrasing;
2. uncommon home-treatment modifiers that can affect the case;
3. rare, fully authored absurd scenarios.

A funny line is not automatically diagnostic, and it cannot be the only path to a critical fact.

## Refusal And Conflict

Financial refusal, denial, demand for treatment without diagnostics and complete refusal are separate situations. Lack of money does not imply conflict. The player can offer a minimum safe plan, staged diagnostics, stabilization, referral, written refusal or later campaign systems such as charity limits.

Immediate satisfaction, trust, adherence, medical outcome and professional reputation are calculated separately. Refusing an unsafe demand may lower satisfaction while preserving medical quality.

## Home-Treatment Library

Home actions are data modifiers with compatible/incompatible case tags, owner lines, discovery actions, clinical effects, trust conditions, rarity and humor level. They can alter symptoms or be irrelevant context; they cannot be attached freely to every disease.

## Dialogue

Short lines are selected by function: complaint, timeline, uncertainty, contradiction, home treatment, budget, internet diagnosis, refusal, anger, anxiety, agreement, misunderstanding, follow-up, gratitude and post-visit complaint. One owner keeps a consistent speaking style.
